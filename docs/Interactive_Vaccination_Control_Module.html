1. Deterministic Control & Herd Immunity

In deterministic models, vaccination reduces the effective reproduction number, \( R_e \). The goal is to reduce the susceptible pool enough so that \( R_e < 1 \). Use the calculator below to explore how the Basic Reproduction Number (\( R_0 \)) and Vaccine Efficacy (\( e \)) determine the critical coverage (\( p_c \)) required to stop an epidemic.

2. The SIRV Model (Routine Vaccination)

Real-world vaccination is often continuous (routine), not a one-time event. We extend the SIR model to SIRV, adding a vaccination rate (\( \nu \)) that moves individuals from Susceptible (S) directly to Vaccinated (V). Observe how increasing \( \nu \) flattens the curve and reduces the equilibrium susceptible population (\( S^* \)).

4. Strategy Lab: Comparative Analysis

We rarely rely on just one tool. Public health strategies combine vaccination, NPIs, and reactive measures. Compare the theoretical performance of different approaches for a Measles-like outbreak.

Baseline (No Control)

Natural course of the epidemic. High peak, rapid burnout, massive burden.

Strategy A: Pre-emptive Routine

High coverage (95%) maintained before outbreak. Prevents epidemic entirely (Herd Immunity).

Strategy B: Reactive + NPIs

Lower initial coverage. Response involves Ring Vaccination and Social Distancing when cases appear.

Strategy C: Targeted

Focuses limited doses on "Hubs" (Super-spreaders). Mathematically efficient but ethically/logistically hard.
