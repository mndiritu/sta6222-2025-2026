Breaking the Abstraction

In previous weeks, we treated "Infection" as a generic state ($I$). Biology was abstracted into $\beta$ and $\gamma$. However, biology dictates mathematical structure. We must categorize pathogens into two distinct classes: Micro-parasites (Viruses, Bacteria) and Macro-parasites (Helminths). Applying the wrong model (e.g., SIR for Hookworm) leads to failed public health policies.

Attribute Profile

Micro-Parasites: The "Binary" World

Examples: Measles, Influenza, Smallpox

Biological Reality

  • Replication: Occurs within the host. A single virion replicates into billions.
  • Timescale: Short generation times (days/weeks).
  • Status: Effectively binary. You are $S, E, I,$ or $R$.
  • Viral Load: Rarely modelled in transmission dynamics.

The Math (SEIR)

$$S \to E \to I \to R$$
$$R_0 = \frac{\beta}{\gamma}$$

Study Tip: When thinking of Micro-parasites, think of States. The fundamental question is: "Which box is the individual in?"

Macro-Parasites: The "Burden" World

Examples: Hookworm, Schistosoma, Ascaris

Here, Prevalence (percentage infected) is a poor indicator. The Gold Standard is Intensity (mean worm burden, $W$). The worm lifecycle involves an environmental stage ($\Lambda$) and does not allow multiplication inside the host.

The "Wormy People" (80/20 Rule)

Worms are not shared equally. They are Overdispersed (Negative Binomial Distribution).

  • • ~20% of people carry ~80% of worms.
  • • These are the "Super-spreaders".
  • • Treating them (Targeted Treatment) removes most contamination.
"If you treat the whole population, you waste drugs on people with few worms."
