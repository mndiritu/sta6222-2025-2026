Notation & Quick Reference

Mathematical modeling uses symbols as a shorthand. This dictionary will help you pronounce them correctly and understand what they mean physically.

Symbol Meaning Notes & Tips
\(t\) Time
"t"		 Usually in days (malaria) or years (TB).
\(dS/dt\) Rate of change
"dee S dee t"		 How fast the number of Susceptibles is changing right now.
\(\lambda(t)\) Force of Infection
"lambda of t"		 The hazard: probability a susceptible person gets infected per unit time.
\(\beta\) Transmission Parameter
"beta"		 Contact rate \(\times\) Probability of infection per contact.
\(\kappa\) Progression Rate
"kappa"		 Rate of moving from Latent \(\to\) Infectious.
Average latent time = \(1/\kappa\).
\(\gamma\) Removal/Recovery Rate
"gamma"		 Rate of leaving the infectious state.
Average infectious duration = \(1/\gamma\).
\(\mu_v\) Mosquito Mortality
"mu sub v"		 Rate at which mosquitoes die.
Average mosquito lifespan = \(1/\mu_v\).
\(a\) Biting Rate
"a"		 Number of bites per mosquito per day.
Pro Tip: When you see a rate parameter (like \(\gamma\) or \(\kappa\)), always think of its reciprocal (\(1/\gamma\)) as the "Average Duration" of that state.

Part 1: TB Models (Latency & Persistence)

Tuberculosis modelling is distinct from the classic SIR model because of the long latent period. To understand it, we start with the "hazard" or risk of infection.

1.1 Step 1: The Force of Infection

Before we write the model compartments, we must define the rate at which susceptible individuals become infected. This is called the Force of Infection, denoted by \(\lambda(t)\).

$$\lambda(t) = \beta \frac{I(t)}{N}$$

This means the risk to a susceptible person depends on the transmission parameter \(\beta\) and the prevalence of infectious people in the population (\(I/N\)).

1.2 Step 2: Building the Model Progressively

Now we can construct the S-L-I model one compartment at a time.

A. The Susceptibles (S)

People leave the Susceptible class when they get infected. The rate of leaving is the Force of Infection (\(\lambda\)) multiplied by the number of Susceptibles.

$$\frac{dS}{dt} = -\lambda S$$

B. The Latent Reservoir (L)

When people get infected, they don't get sick immediately. They enter the Latent compartment. They stay here until they "progress" to active disease at rate \(\kappa\).

$$\frac{dL}{dt} = \underbrace{\lambda S}_{\text{Inflow from infection}} - \underbrace{\kappa L}_{\text{Outflow to active TB}}$$

C. The Infectious Class (I)

Finally, those who progress enter the Infectious class. They stay here until they are treated, recover, or die (at rate \(\gamma\)).

$$\frac{dI}{dt} = \underbrace{\kappa L}_{\text{Inflow from latency}} - \underbrace{\gamma I}_{\text{Outflow to recovery}}$$

1.3 Understanding \(R_0\) for TB

The formula for the Basic Reproduction Number in TB captures both the infectiousness and the probability of surviving latency:

$$R_0 = \underbrace{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}_{\text{Standard SIR part}} \cdot \underbrace{\frac{\kappa}{\kappa + \omega}}_{\text{Progression Probability}}$$

Derivation Sketch (Why is it this formula?)

  • Step 1 (The Competition in L): Once infected, a person is in the Latent state. They can either Progress (rate \(\kappa\)) or Die/Clear (rate \(\omega\)). The probability they progress is the rate of progression divided by the total rate of leaving L: \(\text{Prob} = \frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\omega}\).
  • Step 2 (Infections while Active): If they do become Active, they generate \(\beta\) infections per year for \(1/\gamma\) years. Total = \(\beta/\gamma\).
  • Step 3 (Combine): \(R_0\) is the expected infections from a new entry. Since only a fraction progress, we multiply: (Infections if Active) \(\times\) (Prob of becoming Active).

Part 2: Malaria Models (Host-Vector)

Malaria models (Ross-Macdonald) are more complex because there is no human-to-human transmission. We have two distinct populations (Humans and Mosquitoes) linked by biting.

2.1 Step 1: The Two Forces of Infection

Because there are two populations, we need two separate Forces of Infection.

1. Force on Humans (\(\lambda_h\)): Rate a human gets infected. Depends on biting rate, vector density, and infected mosquitoes.

$$\lambda_h = a b \frac{I_v}{N_h}$$

2. Force on Mosquitoes (\(\lambda_v\)): Rate a mosquito gets infected. Depends on biting rate and infected humans.

$$\lambda_v = a c \frac{I_h}{N_h}$$

2.2 Step 2: Building the Model Progressively

A. Human Dynamics

Humans get infected by mosquitoes and recover at rate \(\gamma_h\).

$$\frac{dI_h}{dt} = \underbrace{\lambda_h S_h}_{\text{Infection from bites}} - \underbrace{\gamma_h I_h}_{\text{Recovery}}$$

B. Mosquito Dynamics

Mosquitoes get infected by biting humans. However, they die at rate \(\mu_v\). This mortality is the most critical factor in malaria control.

$$\frac{dI_v}{dt} = \underbrace{\lambda_v S_v}_{\text{Infection from humans}} - \underbrace{\mu_v I_v}_{\text{Death}}$$

2.3 The \(R_0\) Threshold

$$R_0 \propto \frac{m a^2}{\gamma_h \mu_v} e^{-\mu_v n}$$

Derivation Sketch (Why is \(a\) squared?)

  • Cycle Part A (Human \(\to\) Mosquito): Depends on biting rate \(a\).
  • Cycle Part B (Mosquito \(\to\) Human): Depends on biting rate \(a\) again.
  • Result: Since the cycle involves biting twice, \(a \times a = a^2\). This means reducing bites (e.g., Bed Nets) has a quadratic (huge) impact on transmission.
  • Survival Factor (\(e^{-\mu_v n}\)): A mosquito must survive the incubation period (\(n\)) to become dangerous. If mortality (\(\mu_v\)) is high, this term shrinks to near zero.

2.4 Seasonality

We model seasonality by making the biting rate \(a\) change with time, often using a sine wave:

$$a(t) = a_0 [1 + \alpha \sin(\dots)]$$

Crucial Insight: Disease peaks often lag behind rainfall peaks. Rain \(\to\) Breeding (weeks) \(\to\) Bites \(\to\) Incubation \(\to\) Cases.

Part 3: Data & Estimation Strategies

How do we find the values for these parameters in the real world? This is a critical skill for the exam.

3.1 Estimating TB Parameters

Parameter Data Source Estimation Logic
\(\tau\) (Treatment Init.) Routine Notifications + Treatment Registers Calculate the average "Delay to Treatment" (Time from onset/diagnosis to treatment start). \(\tau \approx 1 / \text{Delay}\).
\(\kappa\) (Progression) Cohort Studies (e.g., IGRA positive) Follow a group of latently infected people over time and see how many develop active TB per year.

3.2 Estimating Malaria Parameters

Parameter Data Source Estimation Logic
\(m\) (Vector Density) Entomological Surveys (Traps) Count mosquitoes per household or per light-trap night. Convert this density to a "per human" ratio.
\(\mu_v\) (Mosquito Death) Mark-Release-Recapture Capture mosquitoes, mark them, release them, and recapture later to see how many survived. Or use "Parity Rates" (proportion of mosquitoes that have laid eggs).

Problem Set Self-Test

Test your understanding of the material. These questions are based on the Crash Unit A Problem Set.

1. Calculating TB \(R_0\)

Suppose \(\beta=4\), \(\gamma=2\), \(\kappa=0.5\), and clearance rate \(\omega=0.5\). What is the \(R_0\)?

Explanation: Use the formula \(R_0 = \frac{\beta}{\gamma} \cdot \frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\omega}\). First, \(\beta/\gamma = 4/2 = 2\). Second, the progression fraction is \(\frac{0.5}{0.5+0.5} = 0.5\). So, \(R_0 = 2 \times 0.5 = 1.0\).

2. Impact of ITNs (Bed Nets)

Insecticide Treated Nets (ITNs) primarily reduce which parameter in the Malaria model?

Explanation: ITNs provide a physical barrier, directly reducing the effective biting rate (\(a\)). Modern nets with insecticide also increase mosquito mortality (\(\mu_v\)), but the primary definition focuses on preventing bites.

3. Seasonality & Lags

If rainfall peaks in April, why might Malaria incidence peak in June?

Explanation: The lag is biological. Rain fills puddles \(\to\) Larvae develop (weeks) \(\to\) Adult mosquitoes bite \(\to\) Parasite incubates inside mosquito (EIP) \(\to\) Mosquito infects human \(\to\) Human develops symptoms. This chain takes time.

4. TB Reinfection Dynamics

In the extended model \(\frac{dL}{dt} = \lambda S - \kappa L + \rho \lambda L\), what does \(\rho > 0\) imply?

Explanation: \(\rho\) represents relative susceptibility. If \(\rho > 0\), people in the Latent compartment (\(L\)) can acquire new infections. This "recycles" them in the system, increasing persistence and making control harder.

5. Combined Intervention Effect

If ITNs reduce biting (\(a\)) by 30% and IRS increases mosquito mortality (\(\mu_v\)) by 50%, roughly how much is \(R_0\) reduced? (Hint: See Q6 in Problem Set)

Explanation: 1. ITNs: \(a \to 0.7a\). Since \(R_0 \propto a\) (linear approx for this Q, or squared in full model, but the problem set solution treated it as linear reduction to \(R_0\)), factor is 0.7. 2. IRS: \(\mu_v \to 1.5\mu_v\). Since \(R_0 \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_v}}\), factor is \(1/\sqrt{1.5} \approx 0.816\). 3. Combined: \(0.7 \times 0.816 \approx 0.57\). This is a 43% reduction (1 - 0.57).
