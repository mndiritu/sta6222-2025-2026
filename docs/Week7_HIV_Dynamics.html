HIV/AIDS Models & Chronic Infection Dynamics

1. Why HIV Modelling?

HIV is distinct from the acute infections (like measles) we studied previously:

  • Long Duration: Infectious period lasts years, not days.
  • No Natural Clearance: No "Recovered" class in the traditional sense.
  • Control: Relies on lifelong treatment (ART) and behavior change, not a one-off vaccine.

This allows us to learn how to model demography (births/deaths), endemic equilibria, and long-term interventions.

2. Natural History (Modeller's View)

Acute ($I_1$) High viral load, high infectivity, short duration (weeks).
Chronic ($I_2$) Lower infectivity, very long duration (years). The main "reservoir".
AIDS ($A$) Immune failure, high mortality.

3. The Core SI Model with Demography

Variables & Parameters

Symbol Meaning Pronunciation (Click to Listen)
$\Lambda$Recruitment (Births) Lambda
$\mu$Natural Mortality Mew
$\alpha$HIV Excess Mortality Alpha
$\beta$Transmission Rate Beta

Equations

$$ \frac{dS}{dt} = \Lambda - \beta \frac{S I}{N} - \mu S $$

"d S d t equals lambda minus beta S I over N minus mu S"

$$ \frac{dI}{dt} = \beta \frac{S I}{N} - (\mu + \alpha) I $$

"d I d t equals beta S I over N minus (mu plus alpha) times I"

Disease-Free Equilibrium (DFE):
Without infection ($I=0$), population stabilizes at $S_0 = \Lambda / \mu$.

4. Basic Reproduction Number ($R_0$)

For chronic infections, $R_0$ depends heavily on the duration of infection.

$$ R_0 \approx \frac{\beta}{\mu + \alpha} $$

Rate of spread / Rate of removal

Logic: Even if $\beta$ is low, a small denominator (long life with HIV) makes $R_0$ large.

5. Adding Treatment (ART)

We add a compartment $T$ (Treated). People move from $I$ to $T$ at rate $\tau$.

  • $\tau$ Tau: Treatment initiation rate.
  • $\beta_T$: Infectivity on treatment (usually $\approx 0$).
$$ \frac{dT}{dt} = \tau I - (\mu + \alpha_T) T $$

Concept: Treatment as Prevention

Increasing $\tau$ moves people out of the highly infectious $I$ state, lowering the effective reproduction number.
